The user message is a single JSON object describing one WLSP (Bunrui Goi Hyo) record together with a list of BabelNet candidate synsets:
- record_id
- wlsp: a text block holding the headword, its near-synonyms, and the category path of the WLSP record
- candidates: synsets, each carrying a synset_id plus text blocks with its lemmas, glosses, categories, and taxonomic relations

One concept is what the WLSP record stands for. What fixes that concept is the headword taken together with its near-synonyms and its category path — never the headword string on its own.

Work through the candidates one at a time and assign EQUAL or NONE:
- Begin at EQUAL in any case where the headword, or one of the near-synonyms, would serve as a natural name for whatever the synset's glosses describe, at a matching level of granularity. Base the decision on meaning — the glosses and categories — rather than on the surface forms; where Japanese data is absent, work from the English or Chinese lemmas and glosses. It is perfectly possible for several candidates to be EQUAL to a single record, so weigh each candidate by itself and never discard one on the ground that some other candidate seems a better fit.
- Move to NONE only where a definite disqualifier can be named:
  1. Broader: what the synset covers is a wider class that merely contains the record's concept.
  2. Narrower: the synset is tied to a single domain, technical setting, or subtype of the record's concept (one profession, one sport, or one apparatus that instantiates it, say).
  3. Shifted: rather than the concept itself, the synset names a part, instrument, agent, result, or attribute of it.
  4. Same string, different sense: although a lemma coincides with the headword string, the glosses set out a different sense. Matching surface forms by themselves never establish EQUAL.
  5. Unrelated, a different concept altogether, or synset data too damaged to read.

A counterpart in BabelNet does not exist for every WLSP record. There are records naming a concept this resource
simply does not carve out, and NONE for every candidate is the right answer there. Coming back with no EQUAL at
all is a legitimate result rather than a failure to decide, and no candidate should be stretched to close the
gap. That remains true where a candidate's lemma is word-for-word the headword: a string that matches while
denoting something different or wider still falls to NONE under rules 1-5.

Your reply must be exactly one JSON object shaped like this:
{"record_id": "<record_id>", "labels": [{"synset_id": "<sid>", "label": "EQUAL|NONE"}, ...]}
Every candidate synset_id appears once, in the order given. Nothing outside the JSON.
