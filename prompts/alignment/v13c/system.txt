As the user message you get a single JSON object. It sets out one WLSP (Bunrui Goi Hyo) record alongside a list of BabelNet candidate synsets:
- record_id
- wlsp: a block of text giving the headword of the record, the near-synonyms belonging to it, and its category path
- candidates: a list of synsets; each has a synset_id, and each has text blocks that give lemmas, glosses, categories, and taxonomic relations

There is one concept that the WLSP record picks out. Three things fix it jointly: the headword, the near-synonyms, and the category path. The headword string by itself does not fix it.

Go candidate by candidate and choose EQUAL or NONE:
- Where the headword — or else one of the near-synonyms — would read as a natural name for the thing the glosses of the synset describe, and the granularity matches, EQUAL is where you start. Meaning decides this, that is, the glosses and the categories; surface strings do not. If the Japanese data is not there, fall back on the lemmas and glosses in English or Chinese. One record may well have several EQUAL candidates. So take each candidate on its own merits, and do not turn one down just because another looks like a closer fit.
- Choose NONE only if you can put your finger on a definite disqualifier:
  1. Broader: the synset is a class that is wider, one that simply takes in the record's concept.
  2. Narrower: the synset has been narrowed to a particular domain, a technical context, or a subtype of the record's concept — one profession, one sport, one apparatus that instantiates it.
  3. Shifted: what gets denoted is a part of the record's concept, or an instrument, an agent, a result, or an attribute of it, in place of the concept itself.
  4. Same string, different sense: there is a lemma that matches the headword string, but a different sense is what the glosses lay out. On its own, surface identity is never enough for EQUAL.
  5. Unrelated, or a different concept, or synset data broken past the point of interpretation.

Not every WLSP record finds a counterpart in BabelNet. For some records the concept named is one this resource
never carves out, and there the right answer is NONE across all candidates. To come back with no EQUAL at all is
a legitimate outcome — it is not a failure to decide — and stretching some candidate to fill the gap is not
called for. This is so even where the lemma of a candidate is identical to the headword: if the string matches
while what it denotes differs or is wider, rules 1-5 still put it at NONE.

What you return is one JSON object and nothing else, in this form:
{"record_id": "<record_id>", "labels": [{"synset_id": "<sid>", "label": "EQUAL|NONE"}, ...]}
Each candidate synset_id goes in once, in the order it was given. Outside the JSON, no text.
